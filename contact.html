§ 02 — What to write

Four things that make the answer better.

You do not need all four. But every time I know what the job is, I can reply with a concrete estimate instead of asking for a call first.

01

What is difficult?

Describe the part of the work that takes time and moves nothing. Reading invoices, chasing leads, answering customers, collecting numbers. This is where a system pays back most.

02

Which systems are involved?

Your ERP, your CRM, your mail, your stock app, your own bookkeeping. I build on top of what you have. It is almost always cheaper than replacing something.

03

When does it need to run?

Every morning when 400 invoices arrive, or once a month? That decides both the price and whether it should be an agent or a fixed flow.

04

What is the budget?

A range and a deadline, if you have them. It makes it easier for me to say yes or no straight away instead of spending three calls on it.

Not sure what to write?

Then write "hi" and a couple of lines about what you do. I read it myself, and I reply with the first question I need answered. You do not have to turn it into a project before we have talked.

§ 03 — What happens next

Three steps before anything is built.

Step 01

You write, I reply

I read the message and reply within one working day. If it is clear what you want built, I send a suggestion for scope and price straight away. Otherwise I ask one question.

Step 02

A thirty-minute call

We look at your week and your systems. I tell you what can be automated, what cannot, and how long it takes. If I am not the right person for it, I say so on that call.

Step 03

Written scope and fixed price

You get a written description of exactly what the system does, and a fixed price. Only once you have approved it do I start. If the scope changes along the way, the price changes with it, in writing.
